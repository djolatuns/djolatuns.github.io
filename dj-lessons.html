Beginner course • Self-paced

Welcome to your DJ class

This is the DJ OLATUNS beginner classroom. Work through the lessons in order, practise each task, and build a short mix as your final project. You do not need expensive equipment to begin: a phone or computer, headphones and legal music files are enough for the first exercises.

How to use this class: read one lesson, practise for 20–40 minutes, save your work, then move to the next lesson. Keep a notebook of what worked and what you need to improve.

Course roadmap

Lesson 01

DJ foundations

Understand what a DJ does, music libraries, genres, energy, phrasing and audience awareness.

Practice: Choose 10 tracks you are allowed to use. Write each track's genre, approximate BPM and mood.
Lesson 02

Know your DJ software

Identify the decks, waveform, browser, mixer, crossfader, channel faders, gain, EQ, cue and play controls.

Practice: Load two tracks, set cue points, and practise starting and stopping cleanly.
Lesson 03

BPM and beatmatching

BPM means beats per minute. Learn to count beats in groups of four and align two tracks gradually. Use sync as a learning aid, not a substitute for listening.

Practice: Pick two tracks with similar tempos. Match their first beats and listen for drifting.
Lesson 04

Phrasing and track selection

Learn 8-, 16- and 32-beat phrases, intros, outros, breakdowns and drops. Choose tracks that fit the moment.

Practice: Mark a clean mix-in and mix-out point on three tracks.
Lesson 05

EQ and clean transitions

Understand low, mid and high frequencies, gain staging and avoiding bass clashes. Make small controlled adjustments.

Practice: Transition between two tracks while carefully managing the bass frequencies.
Lesson 06

Cue points, loops and effects

Use cue points to jump to planned sections, loops to extend phrases, and effects sparingly so the music stays clear.

Practice: Set three cue points and create one smooth 8-beat loop.
Lesson 07

Recording and DJ drops

Plan a beginning, middle and ending. Record a short set, check levels, and place a brief original voice tag without overpowering the music.

Practice: Record a 5-minute practice mix and note three strengths and three improvements.
Lesson 08

Professional DJ business

Build a clear DJ identity, prepare a portfolio, communicate professionally, confirm event details in writing and keep records of income and expenses.

Practice: Draft a one-paragraph DJ bio and a simple service brief. Never promise a booking or result you cannot deliver.

Final class project

  1. Prepare a 10–15 minute mix using music you are authorised to use.
  2. Plan the track order and note each transition.
  3. Record the mix and listen back with headphones.
  4. Check sound levels, clipping, abrupt cuts and timing.
  5. Write a short reflection describing what you would improve.

Completion checklist: clean transitions, sensible track selection, controlled volume, a clear beginning and ending, and respectful use of music rights.
